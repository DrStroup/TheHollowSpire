# Chapter 00 — The Explorer Guild Primer

## 0.3 Discovery

Every explorer enters the Spire searching for something.

Some seek forgotten knowledge. Others search for new routes, rare resources, missing people, or answers to mysteries that have lingered for generations.

Whatever form it takes, the pursuit of discovery lies at the heart of every expedition.

A discovery is more than simply finding something new.

A discovery is something that changes understanding.

The location of a hidden path, the remains of a lost settlement, an unusual species of cave fungus, evidence of an ancient civilization, or a safer route through dangerous territory may all be considered discoveries. Some discoveries reveal opportunities. Others reveal dangers. The most important discoveries often reveal both.

Not every discovery changes the world immediately. Many begin as a single observation recorded in a field journal or discussed around a settlement hearth. Over time, however, these fragments of knowledge accumulate. Maps become more accurate. Trade routes become safer. Settlements become connected. Mysteries begin to yield their secrets.

The Explorer Guild maintains records of significant discoveries whenever possible, preserving information so that future expeditions may build upon the work of those who came before.

Because of this, every explorer contributes to something larger than themselves.

A traveler who identifies a safer crossing may never meet the hundreds of people who eventually benefit from that knowledge. A scout who documents a dangerous creature may save lives without ever realizing it. A researcher who uncovers a forgotten history may inspire entirely new expeditions years later.

The Spire is too vast for any individual to understand completely.

Its secrets are uncovered one discovery at a time.

Every route mapped.

Every mystery investigated.

Every question answered.

And every new question that follows.

### Explorer's Note #4

The greatest discoveries are not always the rarest.

A hidden chamber may be forgotten again.

A safer road may help travelers for generations.

When recording a discovery, ask a simple question:

"Who benefits from this knowledge?"

The answer often reveals its true value.
