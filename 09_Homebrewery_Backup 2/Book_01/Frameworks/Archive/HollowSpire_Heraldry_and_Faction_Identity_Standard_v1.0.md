# Hollow Spire Heraldry & Faction Identity Standard v1.0

## Core Rule
A crest, seal, stamp, sigil, or heraldic mark should only appear when the artifact has an in-world reason to display one.

Heraldry communicates authorship, authority, origin, and perspective. It is not generic decoration.

## Hard Rules
- Do not use generic fantasy crests, generic compass roses, invented stars, or placeholder seals in final assets.
- If a crest appears, it must belong to a canon faction, settlement, order, cult, guild, or institution.
- Unknown, private, damaged, ancient, or recovered documents often should have no crest at all.
- A document may use no crest if that better supports its story.
- New crest creation requires a named faction or owner and must be added to the Faction Crest Registry.

## Explorer Guild Crest Usage
Use the Explorer Guild crest only when the document is plausibly issued, stamped, archived, copied, or certified by the Explorer Guild.

Appropriate examples:
- Guild training manuals
- Official contracts
- Explorer field reports
- Discovery reports
- Guild route maps
- Guild notices
- Guild-certified archive copies

Inappropriate examples:
- Anonymous journals
- Ancient ruins
- Enemy notes
- private letters not reviewed by the Guild
- local village notices unless reposted by the Guild

## Future Faction Heraldry
As Book I develops, each major faction or settlement may receive:
- Crest or seal
- Document style
- Color palette
- Border motif
- Stamp/wax mark style
- Common document types

Likely future entries include:
- Explorer Guild
- Pilgrims of the Lantern
- Emberlight Village
- Lantern Village
- St. Lantern's Cathedral
- Deep King affiliated forces
- Other regional institutions as canon develops

## Design Intent
Readers should be able to infer who created or preserved a document by its visual language. Heraldry is worldbuilding.
