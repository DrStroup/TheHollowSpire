# THE HOLLOW SPIRE

## Campaign Tracking Framework v1.0

### Status

Locked Framework.

### Purpose

The Hollow Spire contains several overlapping progression systems.

This framework separates what players should track from what the DM should track.

The goal is to preserve mystery while keeping the campaign playable.

---

# Player-Facing Tracking

Players should track things their characters could reasonably know, carry, map, or remember.

## 1. Current Objectives

Players track:

- Active goals.
- Known routes.
- Known settlements.
- Immediate dangers.
- Current contracts or requests.

## 2. Contracts

Players track:

- Contract name.
- Who requested it.
- Known objective.
- Known reward.
- Current status.

Players should not track hidden Discovery thresholds behind contracts.

## 3. Maps

Players track:

- Player-facing maps.
- Handouts.
- Route notes.
- Discovered shortcuts.
- Unsafe routes.
- Unexplored branches.

Explorer maps may be incomplete, inaccurate, or contradictory.

## 4. Codex Entries

Players track:

- Visible Codex entries.
- Incomplete entries.
- Observations made.
- Samples recovered.
- Journal fragments.
- Codex chips obtained.

The Codex may hide deeper classifications until later.

## 5. Lantern Stability

Players track expedition-facing lantern stability.

They should know:

- Current lantern stability.
- Which routes have been illuminated this expedition.
- Where known refill/restoration points exist.
- Whether they are risking travel in darkness.

They do not need to know deeper truths about lantern oil.

## 6. Inventory And Salvage

Players track:

- Useful supplies.
- Salvage.
- Creature materials.
- Explorer tools.
- Lantern parts.
- Quest items.

## 7. Companions

Players track:

- Companion name.
- Companion abilities.
- Companion needs.
- Companion bond.
- Current companion status.

Companions are stewardship partners, not captured pets.

## 8. Settlement Relationships

Players may track broad relationship notes.

Examples:

- Emberlight: cautious.
- Lantern Village: neutral.
- St. Lantern's Vigil: unknown.

Players do not track hidden settlement meters unless a table prefers that style.

## 9. Explorer Journal

Players are encouraged to track:

- Questions.
- Theories.
- Contradictions.
- Strange phrases.
- Repeated symbols.
- Unanswered mysteries.

This supports long-term Hollow Spire play.

# DM-Facing Tracking

The DM tracks hidden, semi-hidden, and structural systems.

## 1. Discovery Total

The DM tracks:

- Floor Discovery.
- Region Discovery.
- Campaign Discovery.
- Hidden thresholds crossed.

Discovery is not normally shown as a number to players.

## 2. Discovery Registry

The DM tracks each discovery by checklist.

Required tracking:

- Discovery ID.
- Zone.
- Category.
- Value.
- Mandatory / Optional.
- Related systems.
- Whether discovered.

## 3. Discovery Thresholds

The DM tracks thresholds for:

- Contracts.
- Rumors.
- Guild trust.
- Settlement requests.
- Route tips.
- Archive access.
- Hunt leads.
- Registry caches.
- Companion chips.

## 4. Encounter State

The DM tracks:

- Encounter category.
- Whether encounter is finite or repeatable.
- Rare encounter status.
- Encounter consequences.
- Escalation or de-escalation.
- Whether the encounter affects Discovery.

## 5. Hunt Progress

The DM tracks:

- Hunt seed discovered.
- Evidence found.
- Hunt stage.
- Related Codex entries.
- Rewards unlocked.
- Whether the Hunt has been understood by players.

Hunts are discovered, not posted.

## 6. Codex Progress

The DM tracks:

- Hidden Codex categories.
- Entry completion.
- Samples.
- Observations.
- Journal fragments.
- Codex chips.
- Companion chip eligibility.
- Registry cache access.

## 7. Lantern Network

The DM tracks:

- Lantern shrines.
- Lantern outposts.
- Restored routes.
- Known refill points.
- Stability upgrades.
- Settlement lighting condition.
- Lantern brightness expression.

## 8. Route Unlocks

The DM tracks:

- Shortcuts.
- Hidden passages.
- Keyed routes.
- Moon Lantern routes.
- Unsafe routes.
- Restored routes.
- One-way routes.
- Collapsed routes.

## 9. Settlement Development

The DM tracks:

- Settlement needs.
- Settlement trust.
- Supplies restored.
- NPC availability.
- Contract unlocks.
- Training unlocks.
- Route support.
- Lantern support.

## 10. Silent Jailer Events

The DM tracks:

- Where the Silent Jailer has acted.
- What duty was performed.
- Whether players witnessed the result.
- Whether players understand it as routine rather than aid.

The Silent Jailer is not helping the party. It is performing ancient duties.

## 11. Escaped Entity Activity

The DM tracks:

- Evidence of passage.
- Damage patterns.
- Feeding signs.
- Fear responses from other creatures.
- Containment failures.
- Floor-level threat clock changes.

The entity is a prisoner and survivor, not simply a villain.

## 12. Seven Seals Progression

The DM tracks:

- Seal foreshadowing.
- Seal discovery.
- Seal acquisition.
- Seal virtue.
- Related Warden questions.
- Moon Lantern progression.

## 13. Warden Questions

The DM tracks:

- Which hidden questions have been asked.
- What choices the party made.
- Which virtue/theme the choice touched.
- Whether the question was answered through action, mercy, violence, sacrifice, ambition, perseverance, hope, or truth.

Players should not know they are answering the Warden.

## 14. Recurrence Clues

The DM tracks:

- Déjà vu moments.
- Contradictory records.
- Familiar handwriting.
- Impossible dates.
- Repeated symbols.
- Echoes of prior descents.

No Book 1 content should confirm Recurrence.

# Tracking Philosophy

## Players Track Experience

Players should feel like explorers.

They track maps, supplies, routes, journals, contracts, companions, and what they have learned.

## DM Tracks Judgment

The DM tracks the hidden machinery:

- Discovery.
- Thresholds.
- Warden questions.
- Recurrence clues.
- Entity movement.
- System responses.

## Rule

If the information would make players optimize instead of roleplay, keep it DM-facing.

If the information helps players make meaningful in-character decisions, make it player-facing.
