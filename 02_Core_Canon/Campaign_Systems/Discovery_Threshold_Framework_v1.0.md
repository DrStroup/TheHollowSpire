# THE HOLLOW SPIRE

## Discovery Threshold Framework v1.0

### Status

Locked Core Framework.

### Purpose

Discovery is a semi-hidden progression system used by the DM to reward exploration, curiosity, investigation, mapping, settlement support, hunts, archive recovery, environmental understanding, and meaningful engagement with the world.

Discovery should reinforce the central Hollow Spire principle:

Exploration matters.

### Core Principle

Discovery is tracked by the DM.

Discovery is not normally shown to players as a number.

Players should not see:

- Discovery totals.
- Discovery thresholds.
- Discovery requirements.
- Discovery gains.

Instead, Discovery is expressed through the world.

### Player-Facing Expression

Discovery is expressed narratively through visible and social changes.

The primary player-facing sign is lantern brightness.

Examples:

- A dull lantern suggests new or unproven explorers.
- A steadier glow suggests the party has learned the area.
- A bright lantern suggests meaningful discoveries.
- An unusually strong glow suggests veteran explorers.
- An exceptional glow suggests explorers who have seen things most people never survive.

NPCs may comment on this naturally:

> "Your lantern's burning steadier than it did before."

> "You've been out there a while, haven't you?"

> "That light doesn't come from oil alone."

> "You've seen more of the Warrens than most."

### Discovery As Threshold

Discovery may unlock content when certain hidden thresholds are reached.

Examples of threshold-gated content:

- Contracts.
- Rumors.
- Guild information.
- NPC trust.
- Hidden route tips.
- Archive access.
- Settlement requests.
- Hunt leads.
- Registry cache access.
- Companion chip opportunities.
- Rare material leads.

### Example Use

The party reaches Emberlight with 49 total Discovery.

The Guild attendant has no task for them yet.

Player-facing explanation:

> "Nothing for you right now. Spend a little more time in the area and check back."

The party explores further and gains additional Discovery.

Their total reaches 51, crossing a hidden 50 Discovery threshold.

When they return, the Guild attendant responds differently:

> "Actually... something came in while you were away. You might be ready for this."

The players experience this as earned trust, not as a visible point gate.

### Discovery Is Not Grinding

Discovery thresholds should reward meaningful exploration, not repetitive activity.

Discovery should come from:

- Finding new places.
- Understanding environmental patterns.
- Recovering records.
- Mapping routes.
- Completing Codex observations.
- Resolving contradictions.
- Helping settlements.
- Finding hidden routes.
- Completing Hunt chains.
- Recovering expedition evidence.
- Restoring lantern infrastructure.

Discovery should not come from:

- Repeating the same encounter.
- Farming enemies.
- Rechecking already solved locations.
- Grinding random encounters.

### DM-Facing Discovery Values

Discovery may be assigned in small values.

Suggested scale:

- Minor discovery: 1 Discovery.
- Useful discovery: 2–3 Discovery.
- Major discovery: 5 Discovery.
- Region-shaping discovery: 10+ Discovery.
- Book-level discovery: custom value.

The exact values remain flexible and may be tuned by floor, region, or book.

### Threshold Bands

Suggested hidden progression bands:

#### 0–24 Discovery
New arrivals.

NPC response:
Cautious, dismissive, or neutral.

Lantern expression:
Dull or unstable glow.

#### 25–49 Discovery
Locally noticed explorers.

NPC response:
"You're getting around."

Lantern expression:
Steadier glow.

#### 50–99 Discovery
Recognized explorers.

NPC response:
"Word is you've been making discoveries."

Lantern expression:
Noticeably brighter.

#### 100–199 Discovery
Trusted regional explorers.

NPC response:
"You've seen more of the Warrens than most."

Lantern expression:
Strong, reliable glow.

#### 200+ Discovery
Veteran or exceptional explorers.

NPC response:
"That lantern burns like an old explorer's."

Lantern expression:
Exceptional brightness.

### Relationship To Lantern Oil

Most people believe lantern brightness comes from oil quality, maintenance, or explorer skill.

The deeper truth is intentionally unclear early in the campaign.

Discovery may imply that lanterns respond to:

- Understanding.
- Recognition.
- The Spire's awareness.
- The prison mechanism.
- The Explorer's relationship to the world.

This should not be explained in Book 1.

### Relationship To Contracts

Contracts are not simply posted because players arrive.

Some contracts, rumors, and requests become available only when the party has demonstrated enough understanding of the region.

Player-facing expression:

- A Guild attendant withholds dangerous work.
- A Lanternkeeper begins trusting the party.
- A settlement elder asks for help after noticing the party's lantern.
- A previously silent NPC decides the party might survive a task.
- A contract appears after new reports arrive.

DM-facing mechanic:

- Requires hidden Discovery threshold.

### Relationship To The Explorer Guild

Discovery functions as an informal measure of Guild confidence.

It is not a rank.

It is not a currency.

It is not reputation in the traditional sense.

It is evidence that the party has meaningfully engaged with the world.

### Protected Presentation Rule

Never present Discovery as:

> +3 Discovery

or

> Discovery threshold reached.

Instead, the DM records Discovery privately and expresses progress through:

- Lantern brightness.
- NPC reactions.
- New information.
- New contracts.
- New route access.
- Changed settlement behavior.
- Codex changes.
- Archive responses.

### Design Rule

Discovery should make the world feel responsive.

It should not make the game feel like a checklist.
