# THE HOLLOW SPIRE

## Warden Question Framework v1.0

### Status

Locked Framework.

### Purpose

The Warden is judging the party.

The players should not initially know this.

The campaign asks one hundred hidden questions through play.

These are not spoken quizzes.

They are moral, exploratory, survival, and legacy tests embedded in the world.

The party answers through action.

---

# Core Principle

The Warden does not need to ask aloud.

The Spire asks through circumstance.

The party answers by what they do.

---

# Player Knowledge

Players should not know:

- They are being judged.
- The Warden is recording answers.
- There are one hundred questions.
- Questions relate to final judgment.
- Choices may affect the endgame.

Players may experience:

- Repeated themes.
- Journal prompts.
- Strange phrasing.
- Reflective inscriptions.
- NPC questions.
- Archive fragments.
- Environmental dilemmas.

These should feel like worldbuilding until late campaign.

---

# DM Tracking Format

Each Warden Question should be tracked as:

```text
WQ-###  [ ]  Question
Book:
Floor:
Zone:
Virtue:
Prompt Form:
Player-Facing Situation:
Observed Answer:
DM Judgment Notes:
Related Discovery:
Related Seal:
```

## Fields

### WQ ID
Unique question identifier.

### Question
The hidden moral or thematic question.

### Book / Floor / Zone
Where the question is primarily asked.

### Virtue
One of the Seven Seal virtues:

1. Truth
2. Mercy
3. Justice
4. Sacrifice
5. Ambition
6. Perseverance
7. Hope

### Prompt Form
How the question appears.

Examples:

- Environmental dilemma.
- NPC request.
- Route choice.
- Contract decision.
- Resource scarcity.
- Settlement crisis.
- Enemy mercy.
- Archive contradiction.
- Companion stewardship.
- Personal sacrifice.
- Explorer journal prompt.

### Player-Facing Situation
What the players actually experience.

### Observed Answer
What the party did.

### DM Judgment Notes
How the action may matter later.

### Related Discovery
Optional linked discovery ID.

### Related Seal
Optional linked Seven Seal.

# Distribution Across Virtues

The full campaign contains one hundred hidden Warden Questions.

Suggested distribution:

## Truth — Questions 001–015
Questions about honesty, recordkeeping, evidence, memory, and facing reality.

## Mercy — Questions 016–030
Questions about compassion, restraint, aid, forgiveness, and how the party treats the vulnerable.

## Justice — Questions 031–045
Questions about consequence, judgment, fairness, debt, punishment, and accountability.

## Sacrifice — Questions 046–060
Questions about cost, loss, protection, hard choices, and what the party leaves behind.

## Ambition — Questions 061–075
Questions about reaching farther, claiming power, pursuing discovery, and risking more.

## Perseverance — Questions 076–090
Questions about endurance, repetition, failure, return, and continuing when the Spire resists.

## Hope — Questions 091–100
Questions about legacy, restoration, light, trust, and whether the party leaves the world better.

# Question Design Rules

## Do
- Embed questions in play.
- Let actions answer them.
- Track answers privately.
- Use the same situation to support multiple interpretations.
- Allow players to surprise the framework.
- Preserve ambiguity.

## Do Not
- Announce Warden Questions.
- Tell players they are being judged.
- Punish players immediately for every answer.
- Reduce morality to a binary score.
- Make the Warden feel like a quest giver.
- Reveal Recurrence through the question system too early.

---

# Relationship To Discovery

Some Warden Questions may link to Discoveries.

Example:

- D-001 reveals the prison no longer functions.
- WQ-001 asks what the party does with freedom.

Not every Discovery needs a Warden Question.

Not every Warden Question needs Discovery points.

They are linked systems, not the same system.

---

# Relationship To Final Judgment

The Warden Question Framework supports the campaign endgame.

Possible final outcomes may reference:

- Patterns of Mercy.
- Patterns of Justice.
- Patterns of Ambition.
- Patterns of Sacrifice.
- Whether the party preserved Truth.
- Whether the party endured.
- Whether the party left Hope behind.

Final judgment should consider patterns, not isolated choices.
