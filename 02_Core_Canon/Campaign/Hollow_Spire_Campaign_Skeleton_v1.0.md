# The Hollow Spire — Worldgen Skeleton v1.0

**File Path:** `02_Core_Canon/00_Worldgen/Hollow_Spire_Worldgen_Skeleton_v1.0.md`  
**Status:** Locked Draft v1.0  
**Purpose:** Campaign-wide world generation and reference skeleton for all future region, book, settlement, artifact, seal, encounter, and manuscript work.

---

## 1. Core Campaign Identity

The Hollow Spire is a vast subterranean world of descent, discovery, settlements, ancient mysteries, impossible ecosystems, lost histories, and living civilizations.

The campaign should not primarily feel like a prison escape, a test, or a jail narrative from the players' point of view. Those truths, if they exist in deeper canon, should remain distant, hidden, symbolic, and rarely explicit.

The player-facing experience should center on:

- Hope
- Legacy
- Awe
- Wonder
- Discovery
- Community
- Exploration
- Sacrifice
- Perseverance
- Moral growth
- The feeling that every expedition matters

The Hollow Spire is not waiting for heroes to arrive. It is already full of explorers, builders, scholars, salvagers, settlers, and adventurers. The player characters eventually become legends not because they were the first capable people, but because they accomplished what generations of capable people could not.

---

## 2. Core Emotional Promise

The Hollow Spire should repeatedly create moments where the players realize:

- The world is larger than they thought.
- People live meaningful lives even in impossible places.
- Discovery changes the future.
- The past is not gone; it is waiting to be understood.
- Leaving something behind can matter as much as surviving.
- The deeper they go, the more beautiful and dangerous the world becomes.

The campaign is not about escape.

It is about inheritance.

Every generation descends farther.
Every generation leaves something behind.
Every discovery becomes someone else's beginning.

---

## 3. Player-Facing Truth vs Hidden Meta-Canon

### Player-Facing Truth

To the players and most inhabitants, the Hollow Spire is a world, a frontier, a home, and a mystery.

They should experience it as their journey.

They should not usually be told:

- This is a test.
- This is a prison.
- The Warden is judging them.
- Their journey is predetermined.

### Hidden Meta-Canon

Deeper truths may exist behind the Spire, the Warden, Recurrence, and the Seven Seals. These truths should be revealed slowly, symbolically, and only when dramatically earned.

The hidden canon exists to deepen the campaign, not to flatten the player experience into a simple explanation.

---

## 4. Population Model

The Hollow Spire is not an adventurer-only world. It is a civilization's world.

A person living on Floor 60 is not automatically a legendary adventurer. Many deep settlements include people born there who are too young, too old, too weak, too rooted, or too afraid to leave. Some live and die entirely within their home settlement.

### Primary Population Categories

#### Settlers

People who live, build, maintain, raise families, and preserve culture.

Examples:

- Farmers
- Fishers
- Craftspeople
- Merchants
- Teachers
- Priests
- Healers
- Innkeepers
- Laborers
- Archivists
- Engineers
- Children and families

Settlers are civilization. Without them, the Spire collapses.

#### Explorers

People who travel beyond safe boundaries.

They:

- Map unknown spaces
- Recover artifacts
- Escort travelers
- Investigate ruins
- Take contracts
- Connect settlements
- Establish routes
- Bring knowledge back

Most adventuring parties belong to this category.

#### Descenders

A rarer type of explorer.

Descenders intentionally push deeper into unmapped or poorly understood regions. Not every explorer becomes a descender. Not every descender survives.

The deeper the Spire goes, the fewer true descenders remain.

---

## 5. Fame and Social Scale

The player characters become more famous over time, but the Spire must remain populated by other competent people.

The party is not exceptional because everyone else is helpless.

They are exceptional among the exceptional.

### Fame Tiers

#### Unknown Explorers

Typical of Book 1 opening.

People recognize the Explorer Guild, not the party.

#### Recognized Explorers

Settlements begin to know their names. Contracts may arrive specifically for them.

#### Regional Celebrities

By the time the party completes a regional book arc, common folk in that region may see them as heroes or villains depending on their actions.

Their deeds become part of local rumor, tavern stories, children's games, guild postings, and settlement politics.

#### Regional Heroes

The party's actions have changed a region in visible ways. Maps, trade routes, settlement safety, political structures, or cultural memory may bear their influence.

#### Spire Heroes

Around levels 15–20, the party is known across multiple regions. Their discoveries and deeds have reached beyond local reputation.

They are no longer merely local heroes.

However, they still encounter peer-level explorers, rival companies, ancient contract hunters, artifact custodians, and famous expedition leaders who have survived equally terrible things.

#### Living Legends

This status should not be reached until the conclusion of Book 7.

Living legends are not merely powerful or famous. They are foundational figures whose deeds permanently alter the history of the Spire.

Book 8 begins after the party has crossed into this mythic status.

## 6. Campaign Book Structure

The campaign is planned as eight books.

Books 1–7 cover levels 3–20.
Book 8 continues into epic-level exploration beyond normal mortal expectations.

### Book Progression

| Book | Level Range | Campaign Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Book 1 | 3–6 | Becoming Explorers |
| Book 2 | 6–9 | Reputation and Regional Responsibility |
| Book 3 | 9–12 | Discoveries That Shape Regions |
| Book 4 | 12–14 | Forces Larger Than Settlements |
| Book 5 | 14–16 | Civilizations, Politics, and Ancient Powers |
| Book 6 | 16–18 | The Weight of History |
| Book 7 | 18–20 | Spire Heroes Becoming Legends |
| Book 8 | Epic | Beyond the Known World |

### Book Design Requirements

Each book should include:

- Multiple floors or a cohesive region of floors
- Several settlements to explore
- Moments of awe and beauty
- A complete encounter package
- A complete contract package
- Regional factions
- Discovery chains
- Exploration procedures
- Regional travel concerns
- Major NPCs
- Regional mysteries
- At least one artifact and/or seal recovery
- Consequences that change the region

---

## 7. Region-Based Structure

Books should be organized around regions rather than arbitrary floor counts.

A region may include several floors, many settlements, major exploration zones, and a complete emotional arc.

### Suggested Regional Philosophy

Each region should answer:

1. What makes this place beautiful?
2. What makes this place dangerous?
3. Who lives here?
4. What do they need?
5. What do they fear?
6. What do they believe?
7. What discoveries can be made here?
8. What changes if the party succeeds?
9. What remains unresolved when the party descends?

---

## 8. Seven Seals Framework

The campaign includes seven major seals representing virtues.

The first seal is locked:

### Seal 1 — Truth

Associated Artifact: Moonlight Lantern / Moon Lantern  
Core Meaning: The courage to see clearly.  
Function: Reveals hidden paths, concealed meanings, false histories, and truths obscured by darkness or deception.

### Working Virtue Chain

This chain is provisional but usable until revised.

| Seal | Virtue | Core Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Truth | See clearly |
| 2 | Hope | Continue despite darkness |
| 3 | Mercy | Spare what can be saved |
| 4 | Courage | Face impossible odds |
| 5 | Wisdom | Understand before acting |
| 6 | Sacrifice | Give without guarantee |
| 7 | Legacy | Leave the world better |

The seals should not feel like abstract collectibles. Each should be tied to a major artifact, a regional crisis, a moral challenge, and a lasting transformation in how the party understands the Spire.

---

## 9. Artifact Design Principles

Major artifacts should:

- Expand exploration options
- Reveal new kinds of secrets
- Create backtracking opportunities
- Change how earlier spaces can be understood
- Connect to the seal virtue of their book or region
- Feel wondrous before they feel mechanically useful

The Moonlight Lantern is the model artifact for Book 1.

It should represent truth, hidden paths, and the first major shift in how players perceive the Spire.

---

## 10. Settlement Design Principles

Every book should contain multiple settlements.

Settlements are not just shops and quest boards. They are living communities.

A settlement should include:

- A reason it exists
- A survival strategy
- Local food sources
- Local dangers
- Local customs
- Local leadership
- Internal conflicts
- Children, elders, workers, and noncombatants
- Trade or isolation patterns
- Relationship to explorers
- Relationship to deeper floors
- A visual identity
- A moment of awe

Important principle:

A deep settlement may contain many people who cannot leave. This should create both vulnerability and emotional stakes.

---

## 11. Explorer Guild Design Principles

The Explorer Guild is a campaign backbone, but it should not make the world feel bureaucratic or safe.

The Guild exists to:

- Preserve maps
- Issue contracts
- Recover lost expeditions
- Reward discoveries
- Maintain routes
- Record names
- Support settlements
- Train new explorers
- Preserve legacy

Core motto:

**No One Descends Alone.**

Core belief:

Every discovery belongs to those who come after.

---

## 12. Contract Package Requirements

Each book should include a full contract package.

Contracts should vary in tone and purpose.

Examples:

- Escort contracts
- Recovery contracts
- Cartography contracts
- Rescue contracts
- Salvage contracts
- Diplomacy contracts
- Investigation contracts
- Beast control contracts
- Settlement aid contracts
- Route-clearing contracts
- Artifact research contracts
- Mystery contracts

Contracts should not all be combat jobs. Many should reveal culture, ecology, history, and community needs.

---

## 13. Encounter Package Requirements

Each book should include a complete encounter package.

Encounters should support:

- Combat
- Exploration
- Social play
- Environmental hazards
- Moral decisions
- Discovery moments
- Settlement consequences
- Travel complications
- Rival explorer interactions
- Awe and dread

The best encounters should reveal something about the world even if the party avoids combat.

---

## 14. Awe Design Principles

Every book should contain deliberate awe moments.

These are not optional flavor. They are part of the campaign's identity.

Awe moments may include:

- Impossible skies beneath the earth
- Underground seas
- Lantern-lit cities
- Fossilized gods
- Crystal forests
- Deepborn festivals
- Living machines
- Silent ruins larger than kingdoms
- Gardens maintained for centuries
- Children playing where players expected only horror

Awe should often be paired with vulnerability.

The Spire is beautiful because life continues inside it.

---

## 15. NPC and Rival Explorer Principles

The party should encounter other competent explorers throughout the campaign.

These NPCs may be:

- Rivals
- Mentors
- Peers
- Cautionary tales
- Former heroes
- Lost expedition survivors
- Famous cartographers
- Contract hunters
- Artifact seekers
- Seal scholars
- Deepborn guides

These NPCs should not exist to overshadow the party. They exist to prove that the Spire has history, culture, and a living professional explorer tradition.

## 16. Region 001 Current Anchor

Region 001 remains the starting region.

Working name:

**The Blackwater Warrens**

Book:

**Book 1 — Levels 3–6**

Primary Seal:

**Truth**

Primary Artifact:

**Moonlight Lantern / Moon Lantern**

Primary Player Arc:

The party becomes explorers, learns the procedures and emotional meaning of descent, discovers settlements and living communities, and realizes the Spire is not merely ruins and danger but a world of memory, survival, and legacy.

Player-facing emphasis:

- First exploration procedures
- First contracts
- First maps
- First discoveries
- First settlements
- First awe moments
- First major artifact
- First seal virtue
- First understanding that discoveries matter

Avoid overemphasizing:

- Prison framing
- Test framing
- Warden exposition
- Direct explanation of Recurrence

These may exist as subtle mysteries, not primary Book 1 framing.

## 17. Locked Campaign Principles

The following principles are locked for now and should guide future documents:

1. The players should almost never be told the Spire is a test or prison.
2. The player-facing campaign is a journey of hope, legacy, awe, discovery, and descent.
3. The Spire is a civilization's world, not merely an adventurer's dungeon.
4. Many deep inhabitants are ordinary people born in deep settlements.
5. Explorers are important because they extend, protect, and connect civilization.
6. The party becomes regionally famous by completing regional arcs.
7. By levels 15–20, the party are Spire heroes, but they do not become true legends until the end of Book 7.
8. Other high-level explorers exist and should sometimes be on par with the party.
9. Each book should include settlements, awe, contracts, encounters, discoveries, and an artifact and/or seal.
10. The Seven Seals represent virtues, beginning with Truth through the Moonlight Lantern.
11. Book 8 is epic-level exploration beyond the known campaign structure.
12. The hidden Warden/test/prison/recurrence material should remain deep meta-canon unless dramatically earned.

1. Are the seals literal virtues, or artifacts that teach virtues?
2. Are seals carried, awakened, consumed, or integrated into the Spire?
3. What is the Warden's true nature?
4. What is the final emotional meaning of Seal 7?
5. How many regions/floors should each book cover?
6. What are the exact names of Books 2–8?
7. What are the artifacts associated with Seals 2–7?
8. How public is seal lore among scholars and explorers?
9. What does epic-level exploration mean mechanically and narratively in Book 8?
