# Warden and Recurrence Framework v1.0

## The Warden

The Warden is a watcher and judge.

The Warden observes the journey of those who descend. Whether the Spire is prison, sanctuary, test, world, or something stranger should remain hidden until dramatically earned.

The Warden is not merely a final enemy or prison administrator. The Warden is the figure who measures the party's choices, answers, sacrifices, failures, and legacy.

## The Warden's Questions

Each floor may present the party with a question.

These questions function as a journal of the party's journey.

The answers become part of the final judgment.

The questions are not only tests of knowledge or morality. They are records of what the characters valued, what they preserved, what they destroyed, and what they left behind.

## Final Judgment

At the end of the campaign, the Warden judges the party.

Possible outcomes include:

- The party becomes new jailers.
- The party is destroyed.
- The party discovers or earns another hidden resolution.

Final outcomes should be based not only on survival or power, but on the party's exploration, contribution, mercy, sacrifice, and legacy.

## Hidden Recurrence Meta-Canon

The party may have traversed the Spire before.

They do not remember this.

This truth is GM-facing only. It should never be explained plainly to players in early campaign material and should not be treated as something Floor 1 teaches.

## Echoes of Previous Cycles

Evidence of prior journeys appears throughout the campaign.

Examples include:

- Paintings that clearly depict the characters, but aged beyond what should be possible.
- Journals written in familiar handwriting.
- Echoes of conversations the party does not remember having.
- Murals showing the party in impossible historical contexts.
- Records naming the characters from earlier expeditions.
- Locations that react as though they know the party.

## Purpose of Recurrence

The recurrence system should create mystery, unease, and wonder.

It should not be revealed too early as a simple twist. It should unfold gradually through evidence that feels familiar before it becomes explainable.

## Design Guidance

Use hidden recurrence clues to reinforce:

- Exploration
- Consequence
- Legacy
- Judgment
- The feeling that the Spire remembers what the characters do not
